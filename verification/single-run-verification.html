Single paver run: simulation check

All 9 single-run set-ups passEvery one of the simulated sites was recomputed by hand from its random numbers, and all matched.

What was tested

The app drew sites 2,000 times for each of nine single paver run set-ups covering all four methods. A separate program, written directly from the formulas in WA 0.1 and AS 1289.1.4.1/.2, then recalculated where each site should be from the same random numbers and compared the two. It also checked that no site landed in a clearance strip or excluded zone, that stratified methods put exactly one site in each sub-lot, and that sites spread evenly along and across the run.

“Largest difference” is in metres. Anything under 0.000000001 m is computer rounding, so it shows as 0. “Even spread” is a chi-square test over 20 bins along the run and 10 across it, at the 5% level. “Map / schematic” checks the drawing shows N sites, N sub-lot labels and N − 1 sub-lot boundaries, for the first 200 draws of each set-up. A lot with one sub-lot has no boundaries to draw.

Worked example: WA 0.1 stratified with the excluded zone

The testable length is the run minus the end clearances and the excluded zone with its clearance either side. The sub-lot length is that divided by the number of sites. Each site is r₁ of the way into its sub-lot, measured along the testable length only, so the walk jumps over the excluded zone. The offset is the joint clearance plus r_w times the testable width.

What was fixed: sites not updating when the lot changed

This caused the multi-run problem reported from site. Once sites were drawn, the app only noticed a change of method or number of sites. Changing a run's chainages, adding a second paver run, or changing a clearance or excluded zone left the old sites in place. The map then laid the old sub-lot length over the new lot, so it showed the wrong number of sub-lots and kept every site on the first run.

Before (v1.0.0)Draw on run 1, then add run 2: all 10 sites stay on run 1 and no warning is shown.
After (v1.0.1)
  • Each draw now records the exact lot it was drawn for.
  • Change anything that decides where sites go and the Plan, On site and Report steps show Sites out of date. The site cards are hidden, the old sites come off the map and overlay, and the report is marked NOT VALID until a redraw.
  • Changing the lot back to what it was clears the warning, because the sites match again.

Not covered by this check

  • Lots with more than one paver run. That is the next simulation.
  • Setting out on the ground, and printing on the lab's printers.
  • Lining the overlay up on real drawings. Only the arithmetic of turning chainage into position on the photo was checked.
